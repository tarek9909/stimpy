Before the individual videos, it helps to give all six a shared visual language, so people recognize a Stimpy video within the first second.

**The Stimpy signature system**

The zipper is your visual and sound signature. Every transition in every video should be a "zip wipe," where a zipper line runs across the screen and pulls the next scene open behind it, and every video should start or end with the same short zip sound. Over time, people will associate that sound with you, the same way a sound logo works for big brands. Keep the style consistent: one bold background color per video (matching the Stimpie being featured), one typeface for all text, and a flat, clean illustration style for the hoodie. Everything is vertical (9:16), with text kept away from the top and bottom edges so Instagram's buttons and captions don't cover it.

## 1. Logo Reveal

**Length:** 8–10 seconds
**Goal:** Introduce the name, the look, and the zip sound. This is the brand's first handshake.

**How it plays:**

- **0–2s:** Pure black screen, silence. Then the zip sound starts, and a thin glowing line draws itself from the top of the screen to the bottom, like a zipper being pulled down in the dark.
- **2–4s:** The line splits open like the back of a hoodie parting, and light floods out from behind it. Inside, the Stimpy logo is revealed.
- **4–6s:** The logo's letters flicker through different textures and patterns, one per beat (a graffiti fill, a flame pattern, a pastel print, a checkerboard). It's the whole concept in miniature: the same logo, different identities.
- **6–8s:** The textures stop and the logo settles into its clean, final form. The tagline types itself underneath: "One hoodie. Multiple identities."
- **8–10s:** The zipper closes back over everything with a final zip, leaving only "Coming soon."

**Caption idea:** "Something's about to change."

## 2. The Question

**Length:** 12–15 seconds
**Goal:** Make the viewer feel the problem (too many hoodies, each with only one look) before showing the solution.

**How it plays:**

- **0–3s:** An empty screen. Text appears: "How many hoodies do you own…" A single illustrated hoodie drops from the top and lands with a soft thud.
- **3–6s:** "…just because of the back design?" More hoodies start falling, faster and faster, each with a different back graphic. A counter in the corner ticks up: 3… 7… 12… 20.
- **6–8s:** The pile towers, wobbles, and collapses in a heap. The music cuts out for a moment.
- **8–11s:** Silence, then the pile gets sucked into the center of the screen like a vacuum and collapses into one single hoodie. Its back flickers rapidly through every design from the pile.
- **11–13s:** The flickering stops. Text: "What if you only needed one?"
- **13–15s:** Zip wipe to the logo.

**Caption idea:** "Be honest. How many?" This invites people to comment their number, which boosts reach.

## 5. Stimpie Glimpses

**Length:** 8–12 seconds each, released as a short series (one per design)
**Goal:** Build desire for the designs without revealing them. The viewer should want to see more.

**How it plays:**

- **0–2s:** The whole screen is a closed zipper against a solid color that hints at the design (a deep red, a neon green).
- **2–5s:** The zipper opens just a few centimeters, revealing a thin sliver of the artwork, then snaps shut. It opens again at a different spot, showing another fragment, then closes.
- **5–8s:** The rhythm speeds up with the music: open, flash, close, open, flash, close. Occasionally, a single frame shows a bigger piece of the design, so fast most people will miss it.
- **8–10s:** The zipper nearly opens fully, stops at 80%, then zips shut. Text: "Not yet."

The hidden single frames are the creative trick here: people will replay and pause to catch them, which the algorithm rewards.

**Caption idea:** "Pause it if you can."

## 7. Countdown

**Length:** 5–7 seconds each, posted daily in stories for the final 3–5 days
**Goal:** Build urgency and remind people the launch is close, using one reusable template.

**How it plays:**

The idea is that the number itself changes identity each day, the way a hoodie does with a Stimpie.

- **Each day:** The screen starts with the illustrated hoodie from behind. A Stimpie zips onto its back, and the design on it forms that day's number. Day 5 might be a bold graphic "5," day 4 a "4" built from a pattern, day 3 a "3" in a totally different art style. Text underneath: "Days left."
- **Final day before launch:** The Stimpie that zips on reads "Tomorrow."
- **Launch day:** The hoodie turns to face the viewer, a huge zipper runs down the entire screen, and it unzips to reveal "Live now" with a link sticker.

**Caption idea:** Keep it minimal, just the countdown itself.

## 9. "Which Back Are You?"

**Length:** 8–10 seconds, plus a poll sticker
**Goal:** Let followers pick a favorite before launch, so they feel invested in the reveal.

**How it plays:**

- **0–3s:** Two illustrated hoodies stand side by side, backs to the camera, with their Stimpies shown only as dark silhouettes. Hints leak out from the edges: a glow of color, a bit of texture, a shape.
- **3–6s:** Each silhouette gets a personality label that fades in beneath it. For example, "Loud" versus "Quiet," "Chaos" versus "Calm," or "Night" versus "Day." A spotlight sweeps slowly back and forth between them.
- **6–10s:** Both silhouettes pulse gently in time with the music as the poll sticker appears: "Which back are you?"

**Follow-up story the next day:** A bar fills up between the two silhouettes, showing the results. The winner's silhouette cracks slightly, with light shining through the gaps, and text reads: "You chose. Revealed first." This makes followers feel their vote actually shaped the launch.

## 10. The Split Screen

**Length:** 10–15 seconds, designed to loop
**Goal:** Explain the entire concept in seconds, with no words, in a way that's satisfying to watch on repeat.

**How it plays:**

- **0–3s:** An illustrated figure stands in the center with their back to the camera, wearing a plain hoodie. The music starts.
- **3–7s:** On every beat, a zip wipe runs down their back and a new Stimpie appears. The background color changes to match each design, so the whole screen transforms every time.
- **7–10s:** The beat speeds up and the swaps get faster, until the backs are flickering almost too quickly to follow. The screen splits into four panels, each showing the same figure with a different back, all moving in sync.
- **10–12s:** On the beat drop, the four panels slam back into one, and everything freezes on the plain hoodie. Text: "Same hoodie."
- **12–14s:** One final zip, a new Stimpie appears, and the text changes to "Different you."
- **14–15s:** It ends on the same plain hoodie it started with, so the loop is seamless and people end up watching it two or three times without noticing.

**Caption idea:** "Count how many looks." This rewards people who rewatch.
