## 1. The Concept

Stimpy is a hoodie brand built around one core idea: **interchangeable back designs.**

Most graphic hoodies lock you into a single look. Once the design is printed on the back, that is the only personality the hoodie will ever have, and a new look means buying a new hoodie. Stimpy breaks that cycle.

### How it works

- You buy one hoodie.
- You collect as many Stimpies as you want.
- You change the back design whenever you want, in seconds.
- Without a Stimpie, the hoodie remains clean and wearable on its own.

### Why it matters

Stimpy combines three things into one system:

- **Fashion:** a premium hoodie with a strong visual statement on the back.
- **Customization:** the wearer decides what the hoodie says and can change it to match their mood, outfit, or occasion.
- **Collectability:** Stimpies are designed to be collected. A core collection is always available, while limited drops introduce new designs that don't return once sold out.

> **Hoodie + interchangeable Stimpie = a new look without buying another hoodie.**

## 2. The Product

### The hoodie

A premium, heavyweight hoodie with a clean, minimal design. It looks complete and intentional even with nothing attached to the back. The front stays simple so it works with any outfit and any Stimpie.

### The Stimpie

A printed, full-cotton back panel that attaches to the hoodie with a **one-way zipper**. Zipping a Stimpie on or off takes a few seconds, and each one completely changes the look of the same hoodie.

### Quality requirements before launch

- Wash samples several times to confirm the fabric, print, and zipper hold up.
- Zip Stimpies on and off repeatedly to test durability.
- Check how the hoodie drapes and moves on a body with a Stimpie attached, not just laid flat.
- Confirm the bare hoodie looks clean without visible attachment hardware.
- Make sure the hoodie genuinely feels premium in hand, since the brand promises quality.

## 3. Costs and Pricing

### Unit costs

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Hoodie | $10 |
| One Stimpie | $6 |
| Zippers and velcro | $1 |

### Packs and margins

| Offer | Cost | Price | Profit before delivery, packaging and ads |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hoodie + 2 Stimpies | **$18** (updated 7 Nov 2026) | **$30** | $12 |
| Extra Stimpie sold alone | $6 | **$10–12** | $4–6 each |

### Why these packs

- The original idea of three Stimpies for $40 cost $29 per box and left only $11 before delivery, packaging, and ads, which is too thin.
- Selling extra Stimpies separately keeps customers coming back, which is the recurring strength of the system.
- **The $30 two-Stimpie pack is the main product** (price confirmed 7 Nov 2026). With two designs, customers experience the swap from day one, which drives word of mouth.
- **The one-Stimpie pack is dropped** (7 Nov 2026). The hoodie is sold only as the $30 two-Stimpie pack; extra Stimpies are sold alone.
- **Margin:** at $30 with an $18 cost, the pack leaves $12. **Delivery ($5) is paid by the customer**, so only packaging and failed cash-on-delivery orders come out of it (about $2), leaving about **$10** before ads. Extra Stimpies ($10–12, can be added on the website) are where most of the profit comes from.

### Pricing notes

- Ask the printer for volume pricing on Stimpies, since they will be the most-sold item over time.
- Build delivery costs into the numbers from the start.
- Test demand with the first drop. If it sells out quickly, prices can rise on later drops, which is easier than lowering them.

## 4. The Lebanese Market

### Instagram is where the audience is

- As of August 2026, Lebanon has roughly **3.48 million Instagram users**, about 89% of the population.
- The largest age group is **25 to 34**, making up about 35% of users.
- The platform is still growing, up from about 3.15 million users in August 2025.

### How people buy

- Most Lebanese online sellers operate primarily through **Instagram, Facebook, and WhatsApp**, with fashion sold largely via DMs and stories.
- **Cash on delivery** is the most common payment method, due to limited trust in digital payments and a cash-based economy.
- The ordering flow for Stimpy: discover on Instagram → DM or WhatsApp → pay cash at the door.

### Trust is the biggest hurdle

Many Lebanese consumers have been frustrated by products that look different in person than online, and by sellers who refuse returns. Stimpy stands out by using real footage of the actual product, honest photos, and a clear exchange policy.

### The economy

- Lebanon has become a largely dollar-based economy, with most online transactions in US dollars.
- The minimum wage is about 28 million LBP per month (roughly $313), and average monthly salaries are around $300, so a hoodie is a considered purchase.
- The realistic customer is a young person with dollar income, family support, or a job paying in fresh dollars.

### Competition

- **Budget end:** unbranded hoodies on marketplaces for roughly $10–30, and some local shops with hoodies at $20 or under.
- **Branded local end:** local streetwear brands typically sell hoodies around **$45**, mostly built on Lebanese identity themes (cedars, flags, Beirut, Arabic phrases).
- **Higher-end designers:** a younger generation of Lebanese designers engaging with global streetwear, often with limited production.

### The gap Stimpy fills

Almost every competitor sells a design. **Stimpy sells a system.** No one is competing on a product idea. Keeping the brand in English and away from local clichés positions Stimpy as a global streetwear brand that happens to start in Lebanon.

Limited drops also fit how the local scene already works, since many young Lebanese brands produce in small batches.

### Seasonality

Lebanon's hoodie season is fairly short. Aim to launch in **October or November**, at the start of the cold weather, and expect slower sales in late spring and summer.

## 6. Sales Strategy: Core Collection, Drops and Customization

### Core collection + limited drops

- **Core collection:** 3–4 Stimpies that are always available, so anyone can buy anytime. This provides steady sales.
- **Limited drops:** 1–2 new designs every **4–6 weeks**, in small quantities, never restocked. This creates urgency, gives the page fresh content, and turns Stimpies into collectibles.

### Custom Stimpies: not yet

Custom designs are postponed until regular orders run smoothly, because one-off printing costs more per unit, requires back-and-forth with each customer, and makes quality control harder. Later, custom can launch as a **premium option at a higher price**.

### Community design (instead of custom, for now)

A **"Design the next Stimpie"** contest, where followers submit ideas, the community votes, and the winner is released as a drop with credit, creates the same excitement without the operational burden.

### Saved for the first drop after launch

**"Name the Stimpie":** show one design and let followers suggest its name in the comments, with the winner receiving a free Stimpie.

## 7. Marketing Strategy: Instagram

### Platform

- **Instagram only** for marketing.
- **WhatsApp Business** for orders and questions, since many Lebanese customers prefer ordering by message with cash on delivery.

### Content principles

- **The swap must be shown in real footage.** Motion graphics can explain and build the brand, but real hands zipping a Stimpie on and off is what builds trust.
- Put the key moment in the **first 2–3 seconds** of every video.
- Shoot everything **vertical (9:16)**, keep most videos under 30 seconds, and keep teasers under 10–15 seconds.
- The **founder telling the story** feels more real than scripted actors. Later, customer videos of their own swaps are even more convincing.
- A simple rule: **motion graphics for the brand and the questions, real footage for the product.**

### Posting rhythm after launch

- 3–4 posts or reels per week.
- Daily stories.
- A mix of swap videos, styling ideas, customer photos, and behind-the-scenes clips.
- About a week before each drop, start teasing it the same way as the launch.
- Consistency matters more than volume: choose a pace that can be maintained.

## 9. Motion Graphic Video Briefs

### The Stimpy signature system

All motion graphics share one visual language so people recognize a Stimpy video within a second:

- **The zip wipe:** every transition is a zipper line running across the screen and pulling the next scene open.
- **The zip sound:** every video starts or ends with the same short zip sound, which becomes the brand's sound logo.
- **Consistent style:** brand colors, one typeface, and a flat, clean illustration style for the hoodie.
- **Format:** vertical 9:16, with text kept away from the top and bottom edges so Instagram's interface doesn't cover it.

### 1. Logo Reveal

**Length:** 8–10 seconds
**Goal:** Introduce the name, the look, and the zip sound.

- **0–2s:** Black screen, silence. The zip sound starts and a glowing line draws from top to bottom, like a zipper pulled down in the dark.
- **2–4s:** The line splits open like the back of a hoodie parting, and light floods out, revealing the logo.
- **4–6s:** The logo flickers through different textures and patterns on each beat, showing the concept in miniature: same logo, different identities.
- **6–8s:** The textures stop and the logo settles. The tagline types underneath: "One hoodie. Multiple identities."
- **8–10s:** The zipper closes back over everything, leaving "Coming soon."

**Caption:** "Something's about to change."

### 2. The Question

**Length:** 12–15 seconds
**Goal:** Make the viewer feel the problem before seeing the solution.

- **0–3s:** Text appears: "How many hoodies do you own…" A single illustrated hoodie drops and lands.
- **3–6s:** "…just because of the back design?" More hoodies fall faster, each with a different back graphic, as a counter ticks up: 3… 7… 12… 20.
- **6–8s:** The pile towers, wobbles, and collapses. The music cuts out.
- **8–11s:** The pile is sucked into the center and collapses into one hoodie, whose back flickers through every design from the pile.
- **11–13s:** The flickering stops. Text: "What if you only needed one?"
- **13–15s:** Zip wipe to the logo.

**Caption:** "Be honest. How many?"

### 5. Stimpie Glimpses

**Length:** 8–12 seconds each, released as a series (one per design)
**Goal:** Build desire for the designs without revealing them.

- **0–2s:** The screen is a closed zipper over a solid color hinting at the design.
- **2–5s:** The zipper opens a few centimeters to reveal a sliver of the artwork, then snaps shut, and opens again elsewhere.
- **5–8s:** The rhythm speeds up with the music. Occasionally a single frame shows a bigger piece of the design, almost too fast to catch.
- **8–10s:** The zipper opens to 80%, stops, and zips shut. Text: "Not yet."

The hidden single frames encourage replays and pauses, which the algorithm rewards.

**Caption:** "Pause it if you can."

### 7. Countdown

**Length:** 5–7 seconds each, daily in stories for the final 3–5 days
**Goal:** Build urgency using one reusable template.

- **Each day:** The illustrated hoodie is shown from behind. A Stimpie zips onto its back, and the design forms that day's number, in a different art style each day. Text: "Days left."
- **Final day before launch:** The Stimpie reads "Tomorrow."
- **Launch day:** The hoodie turns to face the viewer, a giant zipper runs down the screen, and it unzips to reveal "Live now" with a link sticker.

### 9. "Which Back Are You?"

**Length:** 8–10 seconds, plus a poll sticker
**Goal:** Let followers vote so they feel invested in the reveal.

- **0–3s:** Two illustrated hoodies stand side by side, backs to the camera, with Stimpies shown as dark silhouettes. Hints of color and texture leak from the edges.
- **3–6s:** Personality labels fade in, such as "Loud" vs. "Quiet," "Chaos" vs. "Calm," or "Night" vs. "Day." A spotlight sweeps between them.
- **6–10s:** The silhouettes pulse with the music as the poll appears: "Which back are you?"

**Next-day follow-up:** A bar fills between the two silhouettes to show the results. The winner's silhouette cracks with light shining through. Text: "You chose. Revealed first."

### 10. The Split Screen

**Length:** 10–15 seconds, designed to loop
**Goal:** Explain the concept in seconds, without words.

- **0–3s:** An illustrated figure stands with their back to the camera in a plain hoodie. The music starts.
- **3–7s:** On every beat, a zip wipe runs down the back and a new Stimpie appears. The background color changes to match each design.
- **7–10s:** The beat speeds up and the swaps get faster, until the screen splits into four panels, each with a different back, moving in sync.
- **10–12s:** On the beat drop, the panels slam into one and freeze on the plain hoodie. Text: "Same hoodie."
- **12–14s:** One final zip, a new Stimpie appears, and the text changes to "Different you."
- **14–15s:** It ends on the plain hoodie it started with, so the loop is seamless.

**Caption:** "Count how many looks."

**Which ads to keep, cut or move money to, and based on what:**

The goal number is **cost per delivered order ≤ $10** (on the $30 pack). At $30 with an $18 product cost, the customer paying the $5 delivery, and about $2 of your own costs (packaging, failed cash-on-delivery orders), each order leaves about **$10 before ads**, so **$10 is break-even on the first order**. Profit comes from extra Stimpies ($4–6 each, at checkout or later). Above about $15 per order, even one extra Stimpie does not pay it back. Everything else below is an early warning sign before enough orders exist.

| When | Look at | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Days 1–3** | Delivery, links, tracking | Only fix broken things: ad not spending, wrong link, Purchase not showing in Events Manager, WhatsApp replies slow. Don't judge performance yet. |
| **Day 7** (~$25–35 spent per ad) | **Hook:** 3-second views ÷ impressions. **Link CTR.** **WhatsApp:** cost per conversation. | Hook under ~20% → the first 2 s aren't stopping people; try a new opening on the same video. Link CTR under ~0.8% → the offer or ending isn't clear. WhatsApp conversations above ~$2 each → change the opening or the on-screen text. These are rough guides, not hard Lebanon benchmarks. |
| **Kill rule** (any time) | Spend vs. results per ad | An ad that spent **$30 (3× the target)** with **no purchase and no checkout started** → pause it. A WhatsApp ad with $15 spent and no real order conversation → pause it. |
| **Mid-month review (~24–27 Nov)** | Cost per **delivered** order per campaign (orders from the ledger, not Meta's estimate). WhatsApp: conversations → confirmed → delivered. | **≤ $10:** winner, move money to it. **$10–15:** keep, test one change (new ending or A3). **> $15 after 3+ orders** (losing money): cut it, move its budget to the better campaign. If WhatsApp chats come in but fewer than 1 in 5 become orders, fix the replies and quick-reply prices before spending more. |
| **29 Nov** | none | Start retargeting R1. |
| **~6 Dec** | Retargeting cost per order | If retargeting orders cost **less** than campaign 1, move up to $2/day from campaign 1 into it. If it has spent $30 with no order, stop it and return the rest to the winner. |
| **13 Dec** (end) | Total delivered orders, cost per delivered order, which video won | The winning video becomes the base for the next month's ads and Drop 02. |

Other rules:
- **Move money between campaigns only at the review points above**, not daily. Changing budgets daily restarts Meta's learning.
- **Raise a winner's budget by at most ~20% at a time.**
- **Count an order once:** website visitor who then orders on WhatsApp is one order. Keep placed, confirmed, delivered and returned in the order sheet, and judge ads on delivered.
- A few orders are a direction, not proof. With this budget the month shows which video and which channel look promising, not a final winner.
- **Push the extra Stimpie:** on the website (customers can add one to the pack) and in every WhatsApp order reply: "Add a third for $10–12?" Also offer it to past customers by WhatsApp. That is where the profit is.

## 11. Brand Identity

### The logo: the split S

The icon is a letter **S** split down the middle:

- **The left half is solid**, representing the hoodie: the stable base.
- **The right half is an outline, dropped slightly lower**, representing the Stimpie: the side of you that changes.

Together: two sides, one identity.

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| **Icon** | The split S on its own. |
| **Wordmark** | "Stimpy" in Poppins Bold. |
| **Full logo** | Icon on the left, wordmark on the right, vertically centered. |

### The three official versions

| Version | Background | Solid half | Outline half | Wordmark |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Main logo** | Coral | Black | White | Black |
| **On black** | Black | White | Coral | White |
| **On white** | White | Black | Coral | Black |

### When to use each version

**Main logo (coral):** signature moments where people should instantly recognize Stimpy: the Instagram profile picture (icon only), launch and drop reveals, packaging, stickers, and the first or last frame of videos.

**On black:** premium and mysterious moments: teasers, countdowns, dark product photography, and labels or embroidery on black hoodies.

**On white:** clarity and everyday use: the website, order forms, documents, hang tags, and light product photography.

**Icon alone vs. full logo:**
- **Icon alone:** small or square spaces, such as the profile picture, woven labels, zipper pulls, stickers, and the signature mark on each Stimpie.
- **Full logo:** anywhere the name has room to be read clearly, such as the website header, packaging, posters, and video end frames.

### Logo rules

- Keep clear space around the logo, at least the width of the solid half of the S.
- Use only the three official color versions.
- Never use a secondary color for the logo.
- Never stretch, rotate, or add shadows or effects.
- Never place the logo on busy photos without a solid background.
- For very small applications (such as embroidery under 2 cm), use the icon alone.
- Have a graphic designer redraw the logo as a **vector file (SVG, AI, or PDF)** before printing or embroidering.

### Primary colors

| Name | HEX | RGB | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coral** | `#FF7A59` | 255, 122, 89 | Signature color. The color people remember as Stimpy. |
| **Black** | `#111111` | 17, 17, 17 | Text, dark backgrounds, premium moments. |
| **White** | `#FFFFFF` | 255, 255, 255 | Clean backgrounds, text on dark, website. |

### Secondary colors

| Name | HEX | RGB | Best used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Electric blue** | `#2E63FF` | 46, 99, 255 | Calls to action: "Order now," countdowns, announcements. |
| **Butter** | `#FFE45C` | 255, 228, 92 | Drop color: warm, sunny campaigns. |
| **Sky** | `#5CC8FF` | 92, 200, 255 | Drop color: fresh, light campaigns. |
| **Lilac** | `#C9B6FF` | 201, 182, 255 | Drop color: creative, softer campaigns. |
| **Cream** | `#FFF4E6` | 255, 244, 230 | Warm alternative to white: packaging paper, hang tags, backgrounds. |

### Color rules

- **Primary colors lead every design** and make up the majority of every post, page, and package.
- **Secondary colors are highlights**, with no more than one or two per design.
- **Each drop can own a secondary color** for its teasers, stories, and countdown, keeping the feed fresh while the core brand stays the same.
- **Electric blue is reserved for action**, so it keeps its impact.

### Text contrast

| Background | Text color |
|---|---|
| Coral | Black |
| Black | White |
| White | Black |
| Electric blue | White |
| Butter, Sky, Lilac, Cream | Black |

### Typography

| Use | Font | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Wordmark and headlines | Poppins | Bold |
| Subheadings | Poppins | Medium |
| Body text and captions | Poppins | Regular |

Poppins is free on Google Fonts. Headlines stay short, punchy, and in English.

### On the product

- **Front of the hoodie:** a small embroidered icon or wordmark on the chest, kept minimal so the Stimpie remains the statement.
  - Black hoodie: white or coral embroidery.
  - White or cream hoodie: black embroidery.
- **Signature details:** a coral touch on the inside of the hood, the zipper pull, or the woven label.
- **Stimpies:** each one carries the small split-S icon in a corner, so every design is signed.
- **Packaging:** coral or black boxes with the full logo, cream tissue paper, and a Stimpy sticker inside.

### On Instagram

- Most posts sit on coral, black, white, or cream backgrounds, with the hoodie and Stimpie as the hero.
- The main icon (coral) is the profile picture.
- During a drop, that drop's secondary color takes over teasers, stories, and countdowns.
- Every video starts or ends with the logo and the zip sound.
